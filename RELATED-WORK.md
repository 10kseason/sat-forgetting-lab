# Related work

These comparisons concern the specified retained interface, the representation of the projected function, and the information available to the algorithm. They record checked antecedents and the scope of the results in the linked sources.

## Exact forgetting and clause elimination

- Lin and Reiter's *Forget It!* gives a model-based definition of forgetting a ground atom in Definition 1 and preservation of consequences independent of that atom in Proposition 3. Lang, Liberatore and Marquis give the propositional version in Definition 8 and Corollaries 5–7. Their Proposition 21 covers pulling an independent retained conjunct outside the existential projection, as in the repository’s separate retained background $B$. [Lin and Reiter, AAAI 1994](https://cdn.aaai.org/Symposia/Fall/1994/FS-94-02/FS94-02-037.pdf) · [Lang, Liberatore and Marquis, JAIR 2003](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.4578) · [Repository background](THEORY.md)

- Rule III in Davis and Putnam is the underlying opposite-polarity elimination rule. It factors the clauses at the pivot and replaces the two pivot groups by the disjunction of their tails, preserving consistency. The repository states the corresponding pointwise identity on the retained variables and uses it for certified traces. Ordyniak, Paulusma and Szeider study a structural case in which each nontrivial resolvent is contained in a parent. Their Propositions 3–4 give the relation to $\beta$-acyclicity and the polynomial algorithm given a DP-simplicial order. Their Theorem 2 gives NP-completeness of recognizing that order's existence. These guarantees concern the specified ordering condition. The repository’s guarded construction bounds intermediate output by a product of inclusion chains and works for any order of the body variables. [Davis and Putnam, JACM 1960, Rule III](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs357/DP60.pdf) · [Ordyniak, Paulusma and Szeider, author paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1104.4279) · [Guarded 2-CNF construction](routes/construction/README.md)

- Marquis's *Existential Closures for Knowledge Compilation*, §3, records polynomial forgetting and polynomially equivalent existential closures for Krom and affine languages in their respective representations. It also gives expressiveness and succinctness distinctions for Horn, renamable Horn and other fragments. This is prior context for keeping an XOR equation as an XOR equation and for requiring ordinary retained-only CNF as a separate output model. The affine-frontier algorithm handles the conjunction of separate 2-CNF, XOR and unate conditions. [Marquis, IJCAI 2011](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2011/0171.pdf) · [Affine frontier](routes/affine-frontier/README.md)

## Horn systems, single heads and positional choices

- Liberatore's *Common equivalence and size of forgetting from Horn formulae* gives a polynomial-space forgetting method for Horn formulas and polynomial time on supplied single-head inputs. In the author's full text, §5.7 and Lemma 20 give the single-head runtime argument, Theorem 14 addresses common equivalence of two definite single-head formulas, and §6 extends forgetting to Horn formulas with headless clauses. The repository's one-rule case with consistently oriented retained occurrences fits this single-head setting after complementing the variables of the oriented body. An independent retained background factors out by propositional forgetting. The repository also allows several bounds per head and then expresses the runtime in terms of the size of the policy product. [Liberatore, AMAI version of record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10472-024-09955-5) · [Author full text, §§5.7 and 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.11152) · [Repository theory](THEORY.md)

- Producing an equivalent single-head formula is a separate problem from forgetting from an input already in that form. In *One Head is Better than Two*, Theorem 3 gives the characterization for semantically acyclic formulas. Theorems 8–9 give the polynomial `SHMIN` test and construction on the interequivalence promise. The article's abstract sentence about two necessary conditions should be read with Theorem 4, which gives an interequivalent counterexample to their joint sufficiency. In *Reconstructing a Single-Head Formula*, the general reconstruction is complete when a single-head equivalent exists and can take exponential time. The author text's §§3.5–3.6 and Lemma 13 separate these claims. [Liberatore, JLLI 2025, Theorems 3–4 and 8–9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10849-025-09428-w) · [Liberatore, JLC 2023](https://academic.oup.com/logcom/article-abstract/33/7/1596/6895542) · [Author full text of the reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.10191)

- Tarski's Theorem 1 supplies the general fixed-point setting for monotone maps on complete lattices. Diekert and Kufleitner's Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 3.6 give positional determinacy and identify the reachability winner's region with the attractor. Its complement admits one positional safety strategy on the whole region. Section 4 treats rank or distance for finite arenas. At a fixed retained assignment the repository uses that same AND/OR structure. A reachability move selects a falsifying rule at a losing head, and the successors of the chosen rule lie in earlier losing rounds. This connection is pointwise in the retained input. The further step of emitting a clause for each relevant policy is a claim about compiling the symbolic input-output function. [Tarski, Pacific J. Math. 1955](https://msp.org/pjm/1955/5-2/pjm-v5-n2-p11-p.pdf) · [Diekert and Kufleitner, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.10121) · [Repository policy argument](THEORY.md#one-choice-per-head)

## Horn bounds, bounded-width envelopes and maximal models

- Selman and Kautz define Horn upper bounds as Horn theories entailed by the input and lower bounds in the opposite entailment direction. Their lowest upper bound is the unique strongest Horn consequence, up to equivalence. Greatest lower bounds can be nonunique. Theorem 1 of the AAAI paper preserves consistency for optimal Horn bounds, which makes their general construction at least as hard as deciding the original CNF's consistency through Horn SAT. Del Val generalizes this framework to clausal target languages. His Theorems 1–3 characterize the lowest upper bound by completeness for target clauses and, with subsumption closure for Theorem 2, by source prime implicates in the target. The repository's conjunction of all entailed clauses of width at most two is the bounded-width instance. Its evaluation count uses unateness and polynomial evaluation, and its recognition result concerns exactness of this bound for the supplied succinct bodies. [Selman and Kautz, AAAI 1991](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1991/AAAI91-140.pdf) · [del Val, IJCAI 1995, §§2–4](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/1995/108) · [Binary and unary exactness](CERTIFICATION.md)

- Kautz, Kearns and Selman's Theorem 1 states the intersection-closure characterization of Horn model sets. Theorem 2 recovers that closure by taking intersections of the characteristic models, defined as the points that cannot be generated by intersecting other models. The paper explicitly distinguishes those generators from inclusion-maximal models. In the repository, testing maximal 2-CNF models is justified by the separate monotonicity of the function being tested. [Kautz, Kearns and Selman, AAAI 1993, Theorems 1–2 and the discussion after Theorem 2](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1993/AAAI93-006.pdf) · [Maximal-model test](FRONTIER.md#from-pair-conflicts-to-complete-assignments)

- Schaefer's Boolean satisfiability classification includes the bijunctive tractable case. In Lemma 21, Böhler, Hemaspaandra, Reith and Vollmer attribute the equivalence of Boolean bijunctivity and closure under coordinatewise ternary majority to Schaefer. Jeavons, Cohen and Gyssens prove projection preservation in Proposition 3.5 and determination by binary projections for majority-closed relations in Proposition 5.4. Unary information is included through diagonal pairs. These facts underlie the pair-extension proof for signed requests to a satisfiable 2-CNF. They also explain why three models with a majority counterexample exclude a 2-CNF representation with existential helpers. The repository's background consistency tests use the usual implication graph and strongly connected components. [Schaefer, STOC 1978](https://doi.org/10.1145/800133.804350) · [Böhler et al., author text, Lemma 21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0306134v2) · [Jeavons, Cohen and Gyssens, Propositions 3.5 and 5.4](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/~richoux/teaching/Jeavons-Cohen-Gyssens-97%20-%20Closure%20Properties%20of%20Contraints.pdf) · [Aspvall, Plass and Tarjan, 1979](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/a-linear-time-algorithm-for-testing-the-truth-of-certain-quantifi/)

## Renaming and bounded occurrences

- Lewis's *Renaming a Set of Clauses as a Horn Set* is the classical whole-formula polarity-renaming problem. Del Val's *On 2-SAT and Renamable Horn* gives linear-time recognition and solving algorithms, and discusses the earlier reductions to binary theories. The repository's condition on pairs of hidden literals is that renaming test restricted to a chosen subset of variables, up to complementing all hidden signs. Assigning all remaining variables then leaves a renamable Horn residual, the strong-backdoor setting. The runtime budget depends on the particular set found by the implementation. [Lewis, JACM 1978](https://doi.org/10.1145/322047.322059) · [del Val, AAAI 2000](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2000/AAAI00-043.pdf) · [Gaspers et al., AAAI 2014](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9111) · [Repository decision procedure](FRONTIER.md#a-complete-decision-procedure-on-ordinary-dimacs-cnf)

- Tovey's Theorem 2.1 proves NP-completeness for mixed width two and three with at most three total occurrences per variable. Its reduction already duplicates occurrences and joins the copies by a cycle of binary implications. Choosing the signs of the clones so their source-clause occurrences are positive is a specialization of this construction. Theorem 2.3 gives hardness for exactly three distinct variables per clause with at most four occurrences. Theorem 2.4 gives satisfiability for exactly $r$ distinct variables per clause with at most $r$ occurrences. The proof matches clauses to distinct variables by Hall's condition. Section 3 gives linear-time decision with at most two total occurrences per variable for arbitrary clause widths. The distinction between width at most three and width exactly three is essential when citing the occurrence reduction. [Tovey, Discrete Applied Mathematics 1984, §§2–3](https://cedric.cnam.fr/~bentzc/INITREC/Files/CB11.pdf)

## Clause shortening relative to a binary background

- Eén and Biere's §3 develops self-subsuming resolution, which replaces a clause by a resolvent that subsumes it. For $C=\ell\lor D$, the repository's conditional shortening has this form when the opposite premise $\neg\ell\lor D$ is supplied by the background's entailment. Piette, Hamadi and Saïs's §3.2, case 2, removes a literal after propagation of its negation under negations of other literals of the clause. Their §4.1 explicitly retries clauses after successful shortening enables more propagation. Han and Somenzi's *Alembic* is the related distillation method cited by Li et al. in §3 of their later vivification work. The repository's single-clause test uses complete 2-SAT under unit assumptions on a separate binary background. In the iterative version, each absorbed clause preserves an exact 2-CNF description of the growing background. [Eén and Biere, SAT 2005, §3](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fbacchus/csc2512/Readings/EenBiere-SAT05.pdf) · [Piette, Hamadi and Saïs, ECAI 2008, §§3.2 and 4.1](https://www.cril.univ-artois.fr/~piette/revival/revival.pdf) · [Han and Somenzi, DAC 2007](https://doi.org/10.1145/1278480.1278628) · [Li et al., §3](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.11061#S3) · [Binary promotion](BINARY-PROMOTION.md)

- Heule, Järvisalo and Biere's hidden literal elimination drops an original literal that implies another original literal through the binary implication graph. Proposition 2 gives logical equivalence, Proposition 5 gives nonconfluence in general, and Theorem 1 gives confluence modulo renaming when alternating with equivalent-literal substitution and retaining the original binary clauses. Their hidden tautology elimination uses binary consequences to extend a clause and removes the entire clause if the extension is tautological. Propositions 1–2 give nonconfluence and logical equivalence in general. Theorem 1 gives confluence for the extended method, which first applies restricted failed-literal elimination and equivalence substitution. The repository's order-independence statement concerns the absorbed clause IDs and the function represented by the final binary background. [Heule, Järvisalo and Biere, SAT 2011, §3](https://fmv.jku.at/papers/HeuleJarvisaloBiere-SAT11.pdf) · [Heule, Järvisalo and Biere, LPAR 2010, §4.1](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/mjarvisa/papers/heule-jarvisalo-biere.lpar10.pdf)

## Literal omission schedules and 2-SAT

- A fixed deletion schedule on $m$ ternary clauses preserves a forced pattern exactly when their positions differ in every coordinate. The corresponding cover consists of translates of $\{1,2\}^m$ in $\mathbb Z_3^m$. Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2 of Bollobás, Janson and Riordan analyze the greedy algorithm for covers by translates, yielding a factor at most $H_{2^m}$ times the volume lower bound here. Kuang and Wang's equations (1)–(2) give the volume lower bound $\lceil(3/2)^m\rceil$ and the analogous classical Johnson–Lovász–Stein upper bound $(1+m\ln 2)(3/2)^m$. Adriaensen, Ihringer, Martin and Villagrán call these skirting sets, prove the Cartesian product property, and give exact small values including $f(3,3)=5$ in their §3 table. [Bollobás, Janson and Riordan, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.3815) · [Adriaensen et al., §§1–3, preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01080) · [Kuang and Wang, Eqs. (1)–(2), preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13252)

- Kuang and Wang's Theorem 1.2 sharpens the existence bound to $f(m)\le 2(3/2)^m-1$ and gives a limiting ratio in $(1.62227,2]$. Equation (3) and Lemma 2.2 applied to the triangle give five explicit schedule words $222,011,101,110,000$. The general upper bound averages the number of even induced subgraphs over a random graph. The repository's pinned instances give forced patterns that persist through single-clause promotion. The volume bound quantifies the number of attempts for the fixed positional schedules applied to those instances. [Kuang and Wang, Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 1.2, preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13252) · [Repository schedule bound](BINARY-PROMOTION.md#a-matching-exponential-base-for-this-solver-after-promotion)

- Moser and Scheder's Theorem 12 and Lemmas 14–15 construct the same type of product cover and use it for a deterministic reduction from $(d,\le k)$-CSP to a family of Boolean formulas of width at most $k$. For odd $d$, they apply greedy set cover to all $2$-boxes on each block and concatenate the resulting block covers. For $d=3$ these are precisely the ternary omission schedules. They use a sufficiently large constant number of blocks, budget the exponential block computation against the output size, and obtain a polynomial factor times $(d/2)^m$ for $m$ coordinates. The repository uses logarithmic block width to make block computation polynomial in $m$. Dantsin et al.'s Lemma 6 uses a related block product for covers by Hamming balls in a local-search algorithm. [Moser and Scheder, STOC 2011, Theorem 12 and Lemmas 14–15](https://www.tu-chemnitz.de/informatik/theoretische-informatik/publications/2011-STOC-full-derandomization.pdf) · [Dantsin et al., Theoretical Computer Science 2002, Lemma 6](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/CourseWeb/Math268_2007WS/DantsinEtal02TCS.pdf)

- Theorem 1.1 in the 2018 MIT 6.S078 notes solves 3-SAT by dropping one uniformly chosen literal per ternary clause and testing the resulting 2-CNF. It uses $O((3/2)^t)$ trials with $t$ ternary clauses. Section 2, Figure 2 of Schuler, Schöning and Watanabe uses partial assignments to a maximal family of variable-disjoint ternary clauses and then solves 2-SAT on the residual. Beigel and Eppstein already give the stronger deterministic time bound $O(n^{O(1)}+1.3645^t)$ in the same parameter. Their Figure 3 reduction makes clause choices into CSP variables, and their Lemma 2 eliminates variables with at most two choices in polynomial preprocessing. The schedule approach supplies a fixed family of 2-SAT subproblems with a known cover property. [MIT 6.S078, Theorem 1.1](https://people.csail.mit.edu/virgi/6.s078/lecture3.pdf) · [Schuler, Schöning and Watanabe, Figure 2 and Theorem 3](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~kyodo/kokyuroku/contents/pdf/1205-17.pdf) · [Beigel and Eppstein, Journal of Algorithms 2005, abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0196677404001117) · [Author text, Figure 3, Lemma 2 and Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0006046)

## Parity encodings and the size bound

- Paturi, Pudlák and Zane's random-order code omits a variable whose value is already forced by a critical clause when it is visited. In the 1999 journal version, §2 Lemma 1 verifies decoding, Lemma 2 bounds the expected description length of a $j$-isolated solution in a width-$w$ CNF by $n-j/w$, and Lemma 4 bounds the number of fully isolated solutions by $2^{n-n/w}$. The proof in the repository uses the same coding method and weights variables by the number of data literals in their own critical clauses after fixing an auxiliary witness. [Paturi, Pudlák and Zane, FOCS 1997](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/PaturiPudlakZane_1997_focs.pdf) · [CJTCS 1999, §2](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/PaturiPudlakZane_1999_cjtcs.pdf)

- Emdin, Kulikov, Mihajlin and Slezkin's MFCS 2022 paper uses exactly the existential encoding model, with $M$ counting clauses and $s$ counting arbitrary auxiliary bits. Lemma 2 gives the exact $2^{n-1}$ bound for no auxiliaries. Equation (1) and §1.1 record the balanced prefix-block encoding with $M\le 4(s+1)2^{n/(s+1)}$ and width at most $\lceil n/(s+1)\rceil+2$. Theorem 1 gives $M=\Omega(((s+1)/n)2^{n/(s+1)})$ as a lower bound, width at least $n/(s+1)$, and the separate $M\ge3n-9$ bound without an auxiliary budget. The exponent and balanced constructions already appear there. Use the numbered theorem for the factors and additive constants. [Emdin et al., MFCS 2022, Equation (1), Theorem 1 and Lemma 2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol241-mfcs2022/LIPIcs.MFCS.2022.47/LIPIcs.MFCS.2022.47.pdf)

- The 2024 journal extension's author preprint treats weighted critical clauses in §3.2 and gives the parity bounds in Theorem 11, equations (15)–(17), with $s=O(n)$ in (15). Its proof of (15) takes $\varepsilon=1/n$ in the intermediate tradeoff. With $r=s+1\le n$, the repository's written argument gives the same exponent $n/r$ and the prefactor $r/2$, which is stronger than the displayed $\Omega(r/n)$ prefactor. The separate linear lower bound of $3n-9$ can be stronger when $r$ is large. The no-helper result and balanced chain upper bound deserve the direct attributions above. The comparison here uses the exact statements in the accessible author preprint and the publisher's bibliographic record. [Emdin et al., 2024 publisher record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00224-024-10168-w) · [Author preprint, §§3.2–3.3](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-3171444/v1_covered_bae46433-048d-425a-b902-131f3abfe3b2.pdf) · [Repository tradeoff and proof](routes/obstructions/README.md#a-limit-for-arbitrary-boolean-helpers)

## Failed assumptions and residual 2-SAT

- Zabih and McAllester's BCP2 iterates one-assumption unit-propagation probes and forces a literal's opposite when the probe conflicts. Lynce and Ouaknine's §§4.2–4.4 state the one- and two-assignment versions explicitly and discuss saturation. A failed pair yields the disjunction of the two opposite assignments. Bacchus's DPLL-BinRes closes binary resolution and propagates new units into the full formula. His hyper-binary rule uses a wider clause and binary premises to infer a binary clause. The repository applies the failed-assumption inference using a complete 2-SAT test on the clauses of width at most two immediately after substitution. The original formulas can contain wider constraints. [Zabih and McAllester, AAAI 1988, §3.1](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1988/AAAI88-028.pdf) · [Lynce and Ouaknine, §§4.2–4.4](https://sat.inesc-id.pt/~ines/publications/aimath06.pdf) · [Bacchus, AAAI 2002](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~fbacchus/Papers/BAAAI02.pdf) · [Repository probe](PROBING-AND-CUTS.md)

- Tseitin's construction puts bits on graph edges and a parity constraint at each vertex. In the proof of Theorem 4.4, Ben-Sasson and Wigderson show that every proper subset of the vertex parity constraints on a connected graph is satisfiable. The repository uses this after substituting up to two edge values, with connectedness of the graph after deleting those edges and with only their endpoints represented in the short residual. Theorem 4.4 gives a width lower bound from middle-third edge expansion. Corollary 4.5 gives Urquhart's exponential resolution size bound on cubic expanders. The repository's statement concerns failure of the specified short-residual test on its cubic family. [Tseitin, §2](https://yurichev.com/mirrors/SAT/Tseitin/Tseitin1968_en.pdf) · [Publisher record of the English reprint](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-81955-1_28) · [Ben-Sasson and Wigderson, JACM 2001, Theorem 4.4, Corollary 4.5 and §6.1](https://people.inf.ethz.ch/emo/SatSem05/Papers/BensassonWidgerson01.pdf)

## Linear triples, Property B and cuts

- The set-property definition in Erdős and Hajnal's Definition 1.1, credited there to Miller, is that some set meets every member of the family and contains none of them in full. For a triple system this is precisely a two-coloring with no monochromatic block, equivalently satisfying positive not-all-equal clauses. Grannell and Knor's §1 gives the projective Steiner triple system on the nonzero vectors of $\mathbb F_2^q$ with zero-sum triples. Füredi and Simonovits's §1 identifies the Fano plane with $\mathrm{STS}(7)$ and records its failure of two-colorability. [Erdős and Hajnal, 1961](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-11.pdf) · [Grannell and Knor, §1](https://www.math.sk/knor/PREP/p083.pdf) · [Füredi and Simonovits, §1](https://www.renyi.hu/~miki/fanofinal.pdf)

- Bilu and Linial's §2, footnote 1, states the NAE triangle-to-cut reduction with two cut edges per satisfied triple. For positive linear triples these triangles use distinct pairs. In a Steiner triple system the triangles cover every edge of the complete graph exactly once. Colbourn, Dinitz and Rosa's Proposition 1.4 uses the equivalent count of one monochromatic pair per split triple. Their §1 credits Rosa with the nonexistence of a nontrivial two-colorable Steiner triple system. The cut version bounds the number of crossing edges by $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$. The repository applies the cut count as a certificate for complementary ternary clauses and uses the usual decomposition of a union of two matchings into paths and even cycles in its local-probe argument. [Bilu and Linial, ECCC TR09-057, §2](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2009/057/download/) · [Colbourn, Dinitz and Rosa, Proposition 1.4](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v6i1r25/pdf) · [Bhattacharya et al., §7.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.04074)

- Darmann, Döcker and Dorn's Theorem 3.6 gives NP-completeness of positive linear NAE-3-SAT even on four-regular instances supplied with a partition of their clauses into four perfect matchings. Theorem 3.11 and Corollary 3.12 extend hardness to each fixed regular degree $k\ge 4$ with that same decomposition promise, and show all such instances with $k\le 3$ are two-colorable. Theorem 4.1 gives a separate dichotomy for linear NAE-3-SAT with fixed exact positive and negative occurrence counts $p,q$ per variable. The threshold is $p+q\ge4$ for NP-completeness and $p+q\le3$ for polynomial time. The complexity statements concern satisfiability of the full triple system. [Darmann, Döcker and Dorn, Theorems 3.6, 3.11 and 4.1, preprint v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03395v3) · [Repository cut certificate](PROBING-AND-CUTS.md#the-certificate-used-by-the-solver)

## Exact transversals in parallel classes

- A parallel class partitions the points into disjoint blocks. A linear triple system is a partial Steiner triple system in Stinson's terminology. A full resolution partitions the blocks into parallel classes. A Kirkman triple system imposes the additional Steiner requirement that every pair of points occurs in a block. Weak coloring forbids monochromatic blocks. In a strong coloring all points of a block have distinct colors. The uniform-majority conditioning used in the repository selects a two-coloring with one minority-colored point per block, so those points form an exact transversal in Eiter's terminology. [Stinson, §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.11033) · [Erskine, Griggs and Širáň, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.04514) · [Eiter, JSC 1994, §2](https://www.kr.tuwien.ac.at/staff/eiter/et-archive/files/jsc.pdf)

- Döcker, Dorn, Van Santvliet and Willger study precisely positive one-in-three satisfiability on a union of $k$ given parallel classes. In §1 they call it $k$-Partitioned 1-in-3-SAT and give its equivalent $k$-dimensional matching formulation. Theorem 5.5 establishes NP-completeness and counting #P-completeness for the linear version for each fixed $k\ge 3$, including the four-class conditioned problem. The repository's example compares this target condition with ordinary weak two-colorability on the same input. [Döcker et al., November 2026 preprint v1, §1 and Theorem 5.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.31145v1)

## Guarded affine systems and pairwise exactness

- A Boolean guard followed by a parity equation is a two-disjunct clause in XOR–OR–AND normal form, with Boolean literals treated as one-variable affine predicates. Andraschko, Danner and Kreuzer define this representation and show in Proposition 3.7 and Remark 3.9 that general 2-XNF satisfiability is NP-complete. Beame and Sun study a CDCL extension for disjunctions of parity equations. These results concern solving those formulas. The [single-equation selector test](guarded_affine.py) asks when the conjunction of all unary and binary selector conflicts captures projected affine feasibility. Its mandatory affine equations are part of the body and are used for row reduction. A further retained Boolean background on selector choices is a separate satisfiability constraint. A failure of body exactness can be excluded by that background. [Andraschko, Danner and Kreuzer, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00733) · [Beame and Sun, SAT 2026, §§1–3](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol377-sat2026/html/LIPIcs.SAT.2026.5/LIPIcs.SAT.2026.5.html)

- Matroid ports describe all circuits through a distinguished element with that element removed. This is the relevant linear algebra viewpoint for minimal inconsistent augmented-row subsets with the inconsistency vector distinguished. Belov and Marques-Silva's definitions of group-MUS and labelled minimal equivalent subset give the analogous bookkeeping when one selector enables a bundle of constraints. On a satisfiable mandatory affine background, pairwise exactness is the ordinary $(2,1)$-Helly property of the family of selector solution sets within that background. Dourado, Grippo and Safe §2 gives recognition results for explicitly represented hypergraphs. An affine description of a solution set is succinct in comparison with a list of its assignments, so the representation matters when comparing those bounds. The checked XNF, matroid-port, labelled-MUS and Helly statements do not supply an attribution for the particular fixed-row consistency and rank-gain test. The targeted searches used conditional or reified XOR, guarded affine systems, minimal inconsistent linear subsystems, graph access structures and Krom approximation. [Martí-Farré, Padró and Vázquez, matroid ports](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v15i1n27/pdf/) · [Belov and Marques-Silva, Definitions 1 and 7](https://arxiv.org/html/1207.1257) · [Dourado, Grippo and Safe, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.12610)

- With bundles, the relevant decision question is whether a pairwise compatible selection can still be globally inconsistent. Kováč's Theorem 1 and Figure 2 are a close antecedent for the 3-SAT reduction's combinatorial structure. The path passes through literal choices for clause stages and forbidden pairs enforce consistency with a truth assignment. Its target is existence of a path in an explicit DAG. The repository's two-row construction uses the inconsistency of an activated affine path with opposing endpoints and asks about semantic recognition of the projected body. Binary CSP is appropriate terminology for relations imposed on pairs of labels. Projection-game Label Cover further requires the pair relation to be the graph of a projection map. General simultaneous feasibility of affine bundles can involve conflicts among more than two labels.
