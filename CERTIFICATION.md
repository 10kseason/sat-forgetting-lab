# Recognizing when the retained function is small

Let $g$ be the projected goals evaluated by the greatest hidden assignment, and keep the retained background $B$ separately. Retained inputs are consistently oriented so that $g$ is increasing. All polynomial time bounds count the declared variables, rules, goals and their literal occurrences in the input size. Equivalently, compress variable names before running the algorithms.

## The threshold from one literal to two

An exact unary CNF is recognizable in at most $n+2$ evaluations of $g$. If $g$ is false on the all-one assignment, it is identically false. Otherwise, for each coordinate, flip that coordinate alone to zero. A false result makes it a forced unit, since this is the largest input with that zero. A true result witnesses that the coordinate is optional. Construct the assignment that sets precisely the forced units to one. This is the unique least model of the unary consequences. Monotonicity makes the unary description exact if and only if $g$ is true on that single assignment. If the test fails, that same assignment shows why. [Algorithm](binary_envelope.py) · [Lean theorem](Reductions/UnaryExactness.lean)

For two literals, all binary consequences are still computable with polynomially many evaluations. However, their models can have exponentially many incomparable minimal assignments. Signed binary representability for the succinct acyclic inputs of this project is coNP-complete. The reduction uses three fresh guards, so the semantic proof also handles clauses with either sign. [Reduction and proof](THEORY.md) · [Lean theorem for signed 2-CNF](Reductions/BinaryExactness.lean)

These statements concern $g$ on its entire oriented cube. If the question changes to equivalence *on models of $B$*, even verifying that the body can be replaced by the constant true is coNP-hard. Move the disjoint matching $H$ of the reduction into $B$ and leave the guarded disjunction as the body. Its unary envelope is constant true, since at least one of three guards is true on every input with at most one zero. The matching entails this body exactly when the original 3-CNF is unsatisfiable. The matching itself has only positive binary clauses. [Formal statement](Reductions/BinaryExactness.lean)

## A checkable positive case with cycles

[The round checker](round_certificate.py) recognizes a sufficient condition without a semantic promise. For a fixed maximum hidden fan-in $b$ per rule and per goal, it checks whether **every reachable head in every evaluated round** and the final output admit binary CNF representations. The background is enforced after certification. Under a successful certificate, conjunction with $B$ and an ordinary 2-SAT solve decides the instance.

Start with the empty CNF for each head, representing true. One rule is a disjunction of retained literals and at most $b$ predecessor functions. If the latter have binary CNFs, distribute the disjunction by selecting one clause from each. Each selection gives a union of the selected clauses and the retained literals. An empty predecessor CNF contributes no selections, because it already makes the rule true. A rule with no predecessors gives the single clause of retained literals. The conjunction over a head's rules is the union of their resulting clause sets.

Consistent orientation gives a simple check at this point. In a positive CNF, a clause is entailed if and only if it contains an existing clause. For the nontrivial direction, make precisely that clause's variables zero and all others one. Thus a binary representation exists if and only if every resulting clause contains one of the resulting clauses of size at most two. On success, retain the minimal short clauses. On failure, take a smallest remaining wide clause containing no short clause. No proper subclause is entailed, so this is a prime implicate for the reported head and round.

With $n$ retained variables there are at most $M=1+n+\binom n2$ possible minimal short clauses. Per rule and round the checker examines at most $M^b$ products; building each union and testing containment costs a polynomial in the original input and $M$. There are at most as many rounds as reachable heads. The complete check is polynomial for each fixed $b$. It can succeed even when the policy enumerator would examine exponentially many choices. In a chain, let $h_1$ be bounded by $(a_1\lor b_1)$ and let each subsequent $h_i$ be bounded by **both** $h_{i-1}$ and $(a_i\lor b_i)$. The goal $h_k$ has $2^{k-1}$ static policy selections. Every round is a conjunction of binary clauses, and the exact output is $\bigwedge_i(a_i\lor b_i)$.

A failure at the final output certifies a genuine wide prime there. A failure at an intermediate head can occur even when the final output is a unit. The regression test constructs a width-three instance with a temporary three-literal prime at round two and final function $a$. Its delayed chain only propagates the unit bound after the wide transient appears. This is the price of the local, polynomial check. [Regression and exhaustive checks](test_research.py)
